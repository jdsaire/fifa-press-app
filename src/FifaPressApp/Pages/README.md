# Pages/

Holds every screen you can navigate to directly at its own web address — as opposed to `Components/`, which holds reusable pieces that screens are built out of but that never have their own address.

- **`Landing.razor`** — the public front door (`/`). What this app is, that it's a demonstration and not a FIFA product, and one way on: browse the matches. It used to weigh two entry points against each other, one of them into the sign-in form; sign-in has a persistent door of its own in the session row now, so the front door stopped needing to be a second one. A signed-in visitor is redirected straight to their record — there's one answer to "where is my record," not two entry points that both claim to be it.
- **`MyAccess.razor`** — the signed-in holder's record (`/record`). What they hold, what changed about it, and what they can do — answered without a tap and without a network. The headline comes from data already on the device, so there's no spinner in front of it, and it always shows how old that data is. Gated: an access record is personal by definition, and the page says so rather than pretending not to exist.
- **`EventList.razor`** — the match list (`/matches`). A supporting surface, not the front door — a fixture list can't answer what changed about your own access. Deliberately shows no access status: that belongs to the person, not scattered across a hundred and four match screens. Public, and its search matches a fixture's round or phase in whichever of the three languages the app is currently in, as well as in English.
- **`EventDetails.razor`** — one match (`/events/{id}`). The fixture and a timezone-stamped kickoff are public; your access to it, and a simulated gate check, only render once you've signed in — stated on screen as an offer, not hidden behind a dead control.
- **`Registration.razor`** — the request screen (`/request/{id}`). Renamed throughout from "Register", which read as account creation; nothing here creates a person, it asks for access to one match. Gated, since it writes to a personal record. On success it returns you to the record, where the resulting change is now the newest entry, marked and animated in as the one you just wrote.
- **`Help.razor`** — help (`/help`). What the service does *not* do, what deliberately won't reach you as a notification, and who to contact — eight independently collapsible sections, all closed on arrival. Entirely static, because the offline path through this app ends here and it has to be readable with no signal; opening a section is browser behaviour, not JavaScript. Public, and the one screen every gated surface points a signed-out visitor toward.
- **`Settings.razor`** — settings (`/settings`). Appearance and language, always; the signed-in holder's name (read-only) and a way to sign out, once there is a session. Public, because how the app is read is not something a simulated session should gate. This is the home the two controls never had — they used to live in the sidebar's nav list for want of anywhere better.
- **`NotFound.razor`** — shown automatically whenever an address doesn't match any of the above.

Each of these files starts with a line like `@page "/events/{Id:int}"`, which is what actually makes it reachable at that address — a plain component in `Components/` has no such line and can't be visited directly at all. That one-line distinction is the whole reason `Pages/` and `Components/` are separate folders, even though every file in both is written the same way (`.razor`, markup plus C#).
