## 2. Minute-scripted agenda

Two days, per the course protocol: divergence on day one with the full group, convergence on day two with a smaller core team after a deliberate pause. The pause is not a scheduling convenience — divergence drains the group, and convergence performed by tired people minutes later is just the last idea winning.

### Day 1 — divergent, full roster (W1–W6)

| Block | Minutes | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 15 | Participants introduce themselves and what they are here to represent. Objective stated. Shared research presented (§3). |
| Warm-up | 10 | *"Describe the message you would have wanted, in the worst moment you've had with accreditation."* Transitions from analytical to creative; surfaces the emotional register before anyone is asked to solve anything. |
| First ideation — Value Proposition Canvas | 60 | Two teams, right-to-left on the canvas for the Amina segment: jobs, pains, gains → products/services, pain relievers, gain creators. Concrete and known friction only. Output written as risk-sorted assumptions. |
| Prioritisation 1 | 20 | Teams present; group assesses and marks what carries. No ranking yet. |
| Break | 15 | Food and coffee. Non-negotiable — the second ideation fails without it. |
| Second ideation — beyond the boundaries | 45 | Feasibility suspended. Ideas permitted to be impractical, expensive, or dependent on things FIFA does not control. |
| Prioritisation 2 | 15 | Simulated Buy a Feature run against the full idea pool. |
| Debrief | 15 | Close the divergent day; transition out of the workshop culture. No decisions taken. |
| **Day 1 total** | **195** | **3h 15m** |

### Day 2 — convergent, core team (facilitator + W1 + W4)

Course protocol: the convergent ideation is run on another day by a much smaller team — the design lead and a few specific experts. W1 is retained as the persona's proxy, W4 as the constraint holder. W5's objection travels forward as a written veto condition rather than as a person in the room, which is a weaker instrument and is recorded as such.

| Block | Minutes | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Data clean-up review | 20 | All day-1 output made legible. Nothing deleted — that is the rule, and the rejected-ideas register in `04_CONVERGENCE.md` is the proof. |
| Second-degree reading of the craziest ideas | 30 | For each, what was it reaching for, and can that outcome be delivered without the impractical mechanism. |
| Convergent ideation | 60 | Anchor the surviving ideas in the organisation's reality without flattening the innovation out of them. |
| Concept-to-principle check | 20 | The concept tested against all three inherited principles and the scope boundary. |
| Debrief | 10 | Close; name what remains unresolved. |
| **Day 2 total** | **140** | **2h 20m** |

**Grand total: 335 minutes across two days.**

## 3. What was shared with participants, and what was withheld

Over-sharing ties participants to what they heard. The rule is: give them enough to aim at the right problem, and nothing that tells them what to conclude.

| Shared | Why |
|---|---|
| The How Might We, stated once and left on the wall | It is the mandate's fixed frame. Without it the group solves a different problem. |
| The scope boundary — visas, vetting, quota politics, MA relationship, guaranteeing access | Participants need to know which walls are real before they spend an hour designing through one. |
| High-level profiles of Amina and Tomás — track, constraints, one line each | Course guidance: share profile-level data, not the research itself. Enough to know who is in the room in spirit. |
| That the service has stages nobody sees into | Frames stage 10 as a target without naming it as *the* answer. |

| Withheld | Why |
|---|---|
| The 22-item pain-point inventory (Run 1 D5) | Handing over a ranked list of problems replaces ideation with sorting. |
| Every number in `04_ANALYSIS.md` §1 — the p-values, the 8/30, the UMUX-Lite means | They validate a pipeline and nothing else. Presented to a room they would be read as facts and would anchor the whole day. |
| Theme names T1–T3 and the codebook | Named themes become the categories participants file their ideas into. The categories should come out of the room, not into it. |
| The P01–P05 verbatims | Vivid quotes from five accounts crowd out six people's own experience — which is the entire reason for inviting them. |
| The carried-forward two-token unknown | Presenting an unresolved research question invites the group to resolve it by opinion. It stays unresolved. |

## 4. Unforeseen twists and facilitation response

**Confidence: [SIMULATED]** — plausible failure modes, not observed ones.

| Twist | Facilitation response |
|---|---|
| **A participant attacks the HMW itself.** W2 argues the real problem is visas and security vetting — people denied entry despite valid accreditation — and that notifications are cosmetic next to that. | Agree on the substance, refuse on the scope, out loud. The visa failure is the most severe thing in Run 1 and the platform cannot touch it; that is why it is a written boundary row and not an oversight. Log the objection in the record, name the boundary row, and redirect to the one edge the platform does own: warning her at the moment her visa class is known. An objection this good is not suppressed — it is answered and filed. |
| **The dissenter takes over or checks out.** W5 either dominates by repeating that his track is fine and needs nothing, or disengages because the problem is not his. | Reframe his position as a design constraint rather than an obstruction: he is not resisting the concept, he is defining Principle 3's upper bound. Give him the explicit job of vetoing any idea that would add clicks or alerts to a bulk coordinator. A dissenter with a role stays in the room; a dissenter who is only tolerated leaves. |
| **Premature convergence.** Within fifteen minutes the group settles on an obvious notification feature and every subsequent idea is a variation of it. | Name it, park it as ID-01, and impose a constraint round: generate ideas that work when the phone has no signal, when the person is already at the barrier, and when the message would arrive at 2am local time. The obvious idea is usually partly right — the risk is that it eats the divergence budget, not that it is wrong. |

## 5. What a simulated workshop can and cannot produce

A workshop is a machine for two things: generating ideas, and making the reasons behind a decision visible and contestable. This simulation can do the second properly — the protocol is real, the constraints are inherited from a documented prior mandate, every idea is recorded with a traceability tag, and every rejection is written down with the boundary row it violated. A reader can therefore audit why the concept looks the way it does and disagree with it on stated grounds. What the simulation structurally cannot do is the first thing. Every idea in this mandate originates from one author reading one prior mandate, so the ceiling on divergence is that author's imagination, and the disagreements between W1 and W5 are disagreements the author already knew existed and chose to stage. A real workshop's value is the sentence nobody planned for — the participant who says the thing that reframes the problem and sends the afternoon somewhere else. That sentence cannot be simulated, because simulating it would require already having thought of it.
