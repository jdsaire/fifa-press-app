## 1. Presentation of the mandate

The prior mandate, `01-design-research/`, ended with one design question and would not answer it:

> **How might we tell a journalist that their access has changed before they discover it by being refused?**

This mandate's job was to turn that question into one buildable concept, and to leave a record of how it got there. It was not allowed to change the question, invent new users, redesign the research, or produce a single screen. It had three objectives: generate enough ideas that the answer was chosen rather than defaulted to; narrow them down against stated tests instead of taste; and hand `03-ui-prototyping` something specific enough to argue with.

---

## 2. Highlights

**One.** The concept is called **Access Record**. It converts accreditation from a decision made once into a record that keeps changing after approval and says so each time — with every change carrying what changed, why, and what the person can do now, sent to both the holder and the venue barrier before the change starts to matter.

**Two.** The prioritisation exercise defunded the very moment the mandate exists to solve. The participant representing the target user spent her budget on infrastructure and abandoned the elimination warning she came for. That idea was carried forward *against* the result, with the override stated. A method that quietly wins over its own purpose has failed, and hiding that would have been the real failure.

**Three.** Nine ideas were excluded, and the three that hurt most — a direct control-key issuance, a FIFA-negotiated press entry channel, a floating quota reserve — were the ones aimed at the most severe problems in the evidence base. They cross boundaries the platform has no authority over. Their rejections are written down with the row each violated.

---

## 3. Methodology

A two-day design-thinking workshop protocol, executed as a simulation. No workshop took place. Six participants were authored, not recruited; every idea, vote, coalition and objection below was written by one person against a prior mandate's documented findings.

Day one, full roster: intro, warm-up, a Value Proposition Canvas ideation on known friction, a prioritisation pass, a second ideation with feasibility suspended, a Buy a Feature round, debrief — 195 minutes. Day two, a three-person core team after a deliberate pause: data review, second-degree reading of the least realistic ideas, convergent ideation, principle check, debrief — 140 minutes.

The protocol follows the HEC Montréal course structure it cites, including the rule that convergence happens on a separate day with a smaller group. What it could not staff were the trained assistants that structure assumes, and it could not experience a no-show — which is one way to tell a simulation from a workshop.

Two ideas that appeared in the pool were reclassified rather than prioritised: one was the mandate's premise, one was an outcome. Neither is a thing you can build.

## 5. Results

**Divergence.** 32 ideas across two rounds — 18 structured against the Value Proposition Canvas, 14 with feasibility suspended. Three of the least realistic were given a second-degree reading rather than discarded; two of the three survived as ideas after their impractical mechanisms were killed.

**Convergence.** Every idea was tested twice: does it serve the question, does it survive the scope boundary. Nine failed and were excluded with reasons; two were reclassified; 21 were prioritised across two matrices and a Buy a Feature round; eight reached the shortlist.

**Concept.** The Access Record absorbs seven shortlisted ideas — the live state object and its change log, mandatory reason and next-step on every entry, emission to the venue access list, volume scoped inversely to a track's human support, urgency derived from change type, entries written for changes that are foreseeable rather than only accomplished, and offline-readable last-known state with a visible timestamp. Three shortlisted or adjacent ideas were left out on stated grounds, including one — a named human contact for every applicant — that is arguably the better answer and is not a product.

**What it does not do.** It does not prevent the reallocation, obtain a visa, or explain the silence before a first decision. The tragic scenario from the prior mandate — accredited, current, and stuck at a border — remains unprevented, and the concept is required not to obscure that.

---

## 6. Recommendations

Ordered. Each is a build instruction, not a suggestion.

1. **Test the premise before building the concept.** Establish whether a usable interval exists between an entitlement change and its first consequence. If it is zero, the Access Record is a better-explained failure and the concept needs rethinking, not prototyping.
2. **Build the log first, and make it the only way entitlements move.** A second write path — an admin override, a bulk correction — makes the record a reporting layer over an unreliable source and kills Principle 1.
3. **Treat reason and next step as schema, not copy.** An entry that cannot populate both is malformed. This is checkable at build time and is the only success metric in the prior mandate targeted at 100%.
4. **Resolve the venue access list ownership question early.** It is the hardest thing in the concept and the obstacle is organisational, not technical. Prototyping must not design as though it were settled.
5. **Implement the track scoping rule as a precondition, not a setting.** It is the veto that stops Amina's fix becoming Tomás's burden, and the dissenter funded the substrate only on that condition.
6. **Carry the two-token disclosure at approval regardless.** The prior mandate's instruction stands independently of this one; it is cheap and harmless if the confusion turns out to be novice-only.
7. **Take the operations recommendation to whoever owns operations.** A named human contact for every applicant is outside product scope and is the single most direct answer to the finding that one track works and the others do not.

## 7. Conclusion, limits and next steps

This mandate produced a defensible concept. It did not produce a validated one, and the difference is not a technicality. Every participant was authored, every vote was priced by the person who cast it, and the disagreements staged here are disagreements one person already knew about. A real workshop's value is the sentence nobody planned for; that sentence is the one thing a simulation cannot generate, because generating it would require having already thought of it.

**What `03-ui-prototyping` inherits:** the concept and its name, three core interactions described as behaviour, a principle check stating what the concept must never do, three boundary edges where users will expect more than it delivers, and six open questions. It inherits no screen, flow, component, label, information architecture or visual decision — all of those are its own to make, and the concept was deliberately held short of them so they would still be decisions.

1. `05_CONCEPT.md` — the concept, the interactions, the boundary edges, the open questions. If you read one file, this one.
2. `00_SCOPE.md` §2 — the inherited contract. What this mandate was not allowed to change, and why.

4. `01_WORKSHOP-PROTOCOL.md` — only if you need to judge the method.
