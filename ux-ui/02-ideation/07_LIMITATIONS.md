**SIMULATED — NOT EVIDENCE**

**Audience:** a professional reviewer who will read this repo and form a judgement about the person who made it.

**Relationship to `00_SCOPE.md`.** That file drew the boundary before the work and is not repeated here. This file defends the boundary after the work: what was decided, on what grounds, and what a reviewer should and should not conclude. Scope statements belong there. Justification belongs here.

## 1. The method actually used

A design-thinking workshop was designed to a professional standard and then simulated rather than run.

No workshop took place. No participant attended, because no participant exists. The six people in `01_WORKSHOP-PROTOCOL.md` were authored — their roles, their stakes, their objections, their budget allocations and their coalitions. The Buy a Feature round in `04_CONVERGENCE.md` was played by one person against prices that same person set. The ideas in both divergence files came from one person reading the prior mandate in this repo, which was itself a simulated study with no human participants, built on a secondary-research evidence base assembled from public sources.

That is the full chain: public documents → a simulated research pass → a simulated ideation pass → this concept. Nowhere in it does a journalist appear. The files were authored with an AI coding assistant, which changes the speed of the work and not one thing about its evidential standing.

## 2. Every scope decision, and why

| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **One inherited How Might We, not reopened** | Ideation that re-litigates its own brief produces a better brief and no concept. The prior mandate chose the one failure point FIFA both causes and controls; reopening it here would have discarded that reasoning to redo it worse, on no new evidence. |
| **No new personas** | Amina and Tomás already exist with documented grounding. Inventing a third would have meant inventing the user whose needs happened to justify whatever the concept became. The one addition — W6, the venue supervisor — is a workshop seat for a documented failure mode, not a persona, and appears in no artifact as one. |
| **Six simulated participants** | The course guidance sets teams of three to four, each holding an end user, a stakeholder and a third voice. Six is two such teams: the smallest roster that seats both end-user archetypes, both organisational sides, the barrier, and a dissenter. Fewer would have dropped a side of the service. More would have been more names doing the same author's thinking. |
| **Two divergence rounds** | One structured round on known friction, one with feasibility suspended. The first is where the participants who doubt the exercise are convinced; the second is where anything unexpected can appear. Cutting either collapses the cycle into a single pass, which is a brainstorm, not a design process. |
| **One prioritisation method** | Buy a Feature was chosen because the roster already agreed on the problem and disagreed about spending. It forces trade-off and exposes coalitions. Running a second method would have produced a second authored result and the false comfort of agreement between two exercises with the same author. |
| **One concept, not three** | Three concepts would have looked like more work and would have deferred the decision this mandate exists to make. A prototyping phase handed three options must choose between them on grounds this folder did not supply. |
| **Concept, not interface** | Screens designed here would arrive at `03-ui-prototyping` as decisions already taken, dressed as inputs. The concept was held at behaviour level so that flows, labels, architecture and visual decisions are still genuinely open. |
| **The ID-06 override, stated** | The prioritisation round defunded the elimination warning — the single idea closest to the question the mandate was built around. It was carried into the shortlist against the result, in the open. Silently reinstating it would have been the more serious failure; so would have been dropping the mandate's own central scenario to protect the appearance of a clean method. |

## 3. What this legitimately supports

- **A defensible concept rationale.** Every element of the Access Record traces to a shortlisted idea, every shortlisted idea to an assessment test, and every test to a constraint inherited from a documented prior mandate.
- **A traceable idea-to-decision chain.** Thirty-two ideas carry permanent IDs from the moment they were written to the moment they were absorbed, excluded or reclassified. No idea disappears without a reason attached to it.
- **An explicit scope boundary that was enforced against good ideas.** Three of the most valuable ideas in the pool were rejected, each naming the row it violated. A boundary that only ever excludes weak ideas has not been tested.
- **A prototyping brief with live questions in it.** Six open questions travel forward, including one that could invalidate the concept outright. They are stated as questions, not as risks to be managed.
- **Evidence that the person who made this can run the method.** The protocol, the canvas, the second-degree reading, the two matrices, the prioritisation round and the convergent pass are executed as they are taught, and the places where the simulation could not staff the method are named rather than papered over.

---

## 4. What this does not support

- **Any claim about what real journalists would choose.** Not one preference expressed in this folder belongs to anybody.
- **Any validated prioritisation.** The budgets, the prices, the coalitions and the two funded rounds are properties of an authored file. That ID-02 outscored ID-06 reflects a pricing decision, not a judgement about value.
- **Any evidence that this is the *right* concept.** It is a defensible one. A different competent person working from the same inputs could reach a different concept and defend it equally well, and nothing here would settle the disagreement.
- **Any claim that the divergence was rich.** The ceiling on thirty-two ideas is one author's imagination. The staged disagreement between W1 and W5 is a disagreement that author already knew about.
- **Any assurance that the concept is buildable.** Its hardest element depends on an inter-organisational boundary nobody in this project has spoken to anyone about.
- **Any claim about the concept's premise.** Whether a usable window exists between a change and its consequence is unestablished. If it does not, the concept explains a failure better rather than preventing one.

---

## 5. Why this was the correct trade-off

This repository exists to demonstrate development capability. The research and design mandates are an add-on: they exist so the build is defensible rather than arbitrary, and so a reviewer can see that the person who wrote the code knew why the product looks the way it does.

Against that objective, a fully resourced ideation programme was not available and pretending otherwise was not an option. The two honest paths were to skip the design phase and build on instinct, or to run the method properly at simulated scale and be exact about what that produces. The first would have left the build with no rationale. The second produces a concept with a complete audit trail and a stated ceiling on what it proves — which is precisely what an add-on is supposed to deliver.

The alternative failure — running the same simulation and presenting its outputs as findings, its vote counts as validation, its personas as users — would have produced a folder that looks stronger and is worth less than nothing, because every claim in it would be false and a competent reviewer would find that out in the first five minutes.

---

## 6. Closing

The value of this folder is not that it discovered what journalists need. It could not, and it says so on every file it produced. Its value is that it makes a chain of reasoning fully inspectable: a documented failure point, thirty-two ideas with permanent identifiers, two explicit tests applied to every one of them, nine rejections each naming the boundary it crossed, a prioritisation exercise whose result was overridden in the open when it defunded the mandate's own purpose, and a concept that states in writing what it will never do and where users will expect more of it than it delivers. Every one of those decisions is available to be disagreed with on stated grounds. That is the property this repository is offering — not certainty about a user population, which nothing at this scale could honestly claim, but a design decision that can be audited, contested, and overturned by better evidence the moment better evidence exists.
