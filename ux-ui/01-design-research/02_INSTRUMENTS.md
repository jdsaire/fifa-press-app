# 02 — INSTRUMENTS

**Repo path:** `/Design Research/02_INSTRUMENTS.md`
**Serves:** the Gate 1 goal — what a journalist needs to be told, and how fast, when access they thought was settled falls apart.
**Status:** These instruments were never administered to anyone. They are written as if for real fielding so that Gate 3 has a genuine instrument to generate data against, and so a reader can judge the quality of the questions independently of the fact that no one answered them.

---

## 1. Screener — 4 items

| # | Item | Response options | Disqualification logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | In your work, do you apply for media accreditation to cover major football tournaments — for yourself, or on behalf of other people? | Yes, for myself · Yes, for others · Both · No · I work on the organiser or federation side of accreditation | **DQ** on "No" and on "organiser or federation side". Organiser-side staff are a different study with different questions. |
| S2 | Which of these tournaments have you covered in person? (select all) | 2018 · 2022 · 2026 · Another major international football tournament · None of these | **DQ** if "None of these" is the only selection. |
| S3 | For the most recent one you covered, how involved were you in getting the accreditation itself? | I submitted my own application · I handled applications for a team · Someone else handled it and I was told the outcome · Don't recall | **DQ** on "Someone else handled it" and "Don't recall". The study asks about the experience of the process, not of the badge. |
| S4 | Could you take part in an interview in English, Spanish or Portuguese? | English · Spanish · Portuguese · None of these | **DQ** on "None of these" — v1 is trilingual and the instruments exist only in those three. |

**Deliberate non-screener.** Nothing here screens for having been rejected, reallocated or turned away. Recruiting only people who hit a failure would guarantee that failures dominate the findings and would make H1 unfalsifiable by construction. People who found the process fine are eligible, wanted, and expected to appear in the data.

## 2. Consent notice

> This study is about how media accreditation is communicated to the people applying for it — what you were told, when, and whether it was enough to act on. It takes about 30 minutes for an interview, or 8 minutes for the survey. There are no right answers and nothing you say will affect any accreditation you hold or apply for; we are not FIFA, not a federation, and cannot influence any application. Interviews are recorded only so we can write them up accurately; recordings are deleted after transcription. Nothing you say will be published with your name, your employer's name, or any detail that would identify you — quotes appear under a code such as P01. You can skip any question, ask for the recording to stop, or withdraw entirely at any point up to publication, without giving a reason and without consequence. Taking part means you agree to these terms.

## 3. Survey — 12 items

**Scales used.** Frequency = 5-point (Never · Rarely · Sometimes · Often · Always). Agreement = 7-point (Strongly disagree → Strongly agree). Others are stated per item.

| # | Item wording | Type / anchors | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Imagine your accreditation has been approved and you are holding the card. On its own, does that card let you into the pitch-side photo positions for a match that day? | Yes · No · Not sure *(correct answer: No)* | **H2** |
| Q2 | Before you applied, how much of the organiser's published accreditation guidance did you read? | Didn't read it · Skimmed it · Read most of it · Read it closely | **H2** |
| Q3 | While covering your most recent tournament, how many times did you have to ask someone — a colleague, your federation, the organiser — what your accreditation actually allowed you to do? | Not once · 1–2 times · 3–5 times · 6 or more | **H2** |
| Q4 | How long did you wait between submitting your application and getting a final answer? | Under 2 weeks · 2–4 weeks · 1–2 months · Over 2 months · Never got a clear final answer | **H1** |
| Q5 | During that wait, how often could you see where your application stood? | Frequency (5-point) | **H1** |
| Q6 | Suppose the answer was going to be a refusal either way. Which would you rather receive? | (a) A refusal in one week, with no reason given · (b) A refusal in three weeks, with the specific reason and what you could do next | **H1** *(the item that can disconfirm H1)* |
| Q7 | If you were refused, which of these would be enough for you? | (a) "Your application was not successful." · (b) "Not successful — your federation's quota was full." · (c) "Not successful — quota full; 14 applicants were ahead of you; places may reopen after the group stage." · (d) The full internal decision record | **H1** |
| Q8 | Has your access ever changed after it had already been confirmed — a place reassigned, a match request refused, a quota altered mid-tournament? | Yes · No · Not sure | **H1** |
| Q9 | The last time that happened, how did you find out? (select all) | An official message from the organiser · My federation told me · A colleague told me · I found out at the gate · I was never told officially · Hasn't happened to me | **H1** |
| Q10 | If your access changes during a tournament, how soon should you be told? | Within minutes · Same day · Within 24 hours · Any time before my next match · It doesn't need to be fast, only clear | **H1** |
| Q11 | The organiser's accreditation system's capabilities meet my needs. | Agreement (7-point) — UMUX-Lite | **H1 / H2** context |
| Q12 | The organiser's accreditation system is easy to use. | Agreement (7-point) — UMUX-Lite | **H1 / H2** context |

**Why UMUX-Lite is only two items.** Q11 and Q12 exist to give a single usability reading of the incumbent system that the qualitative material can be read against. Two items is the whole instrument; adding more would imply a benchmark this study cannot produce.

### Translation risks — EN / ES / PT

Noted only where the item genuinely breaks, not as a general caution.

| Item | Risk |
|---|---|
| Q1 | In Spanish and Portuguese, *acreditación / credencial* is used colloquially for the entire right to be there, so the question can read as tautological ("does your access let you in?"). ES and PT versions must name the two objects separately and concretely — the card versus the per-match entry — rather than translating "accreditation card" literally. |
| Q7, Q8 | "Quota" translates to *cupo* (PT *cota*), but Spanish *cuota* means a **fee**. A wrong-cognate translation turns a question about places into a question about money. |
| Q11 | UMUX-Lite's standard "meet my requirements" becomes *requisitos* / *requisitos legais* in both languages, which reads as contractual or legal compliance rather than personal need. Rendered as needs (*mis necesidades* / *minhas necessidades*) — a documented deviation from the canonical wording, accepted because the literal version measures the wrong thing. |
| Q10 | "Before my next match" is ambiguous for anyone covering two matches in a day; ES and PT versions specify the next match *you are due to cover*. |

## 4. Interview guide

**Format:** semi-structured, ~30 minutes, one participant. Follow the participant's order if they jump ahead; the five core questions must all be covered before closing.

### Warm-up (3 min)
Tell me what you cover and who you file for. Then: think back to the last tournament you covered — what was the first thing you had to do to be allowed in the building?

*Purpose: establishes role and track (federation quota, agency-direct, rights-holder, host-city) without asking them to self-classify.*

### Core questions

**C1 — The application, start to finish.**
Walk me through your most recent application from the moment you knew you'd be going to the moment you had an answer.
*Probes:* Who gave you the code or key you needed, and how long did that take? · Was there any point where you didn't know what was happening? · What did you do during that gap? · Did you ever have to chase someone?

**C2 — A refusal.** *(Gap 4)*
Tell me about a time you, or someone on your team, was turned down.
*Probes:* How did you find out? · What reason were you given, in the exact words used? · What did you do next — was there anyone to ask? · What would have made that message useful rather than just final? · If it never happened to you: what do you think you'd do, and who would you call first?

**C3 — Access changing mid-tournament.** *(Gap 7)*
Once a tournament is underway, has your access ever changed after it was already confirmed?
*Probes:* What changed, and at what point in the tournament? · How did you learn about it — and how long after the change? · What did it cost you: a match, a flight, a story? · How much warning would have made it manageable? · Did you understand why it happened?

**C4 — The gate.** *(Gap 5)*
Describe the moment of actually getting into a venue on a matchday.
*Probes:* What do you show, and to whom? · Has anyone ever told you that you couldn't go somewhere you expected to be able to go? · What did they say, and did it match what you'd been told beforehand? · Where were you standing when you found out? · Phone, card, or both — and what happens when the signal drops?

**C5 — What should have been said.** *(Serves the goal directly)*
Take whichever of those moments was worst for you. If you could rewrite the message you got — what should it have said, and when should it have reached you?
*Probes:* Who should it have come from? · How much detail is too much? · Is there a point where more information stops helping? · **Disconfirming probe:** is there any part of this you'd rather they left alone — anything that works fine now, or any message you'd rather not receive at all?

### Closing (2 min)
What's the one thing about this process you'd change if you could change only one? · Is there anything I should have asked and didn't? · Is there someone else doing this job differently from you that I ought to speak to?
